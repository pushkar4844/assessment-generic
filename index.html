01 Opening Introduction

How to open the call and set the tone.

The Intro

First 2 minutes — set the tone
"Hi everyone, thank you for taking the time today. My name is Pushkar Patil — I'm a Cloud Engineer with AWS Professional Services. I specialize in network architecture and security for cloud environments, and I've been working with customers on AWS infrastructure design, security posture assessments, and internet breakout architectures."
"Before we jump into the technical details, I want to take a couple of minutes to set the context for what we're doing here and how we'll work together."

Build Trust — Show You Understand Their World

Next 3 minutes
"I understand you're looking at your AWS Cloud Internet Breakout environment — how your workloads connect to the internet, how that traffic is secured, and whether the architecture is aligned with best practices. This is one of the most critical areas in any cloud environment because it's where your internal workloads meet the public internet. Getting this right has a direct impact on your security posture, compliance readiness, and operational resilience."
"I want to be upfront about how we approach this — this is NOT an audit. We're not here to grade you or find fault. We're here as your partner to give you a clear, honest picture of where things stand today, and more importantly, a practical roadmap to get where you want to be."
"Every organization has gaps — that's normal, especially as cloud environments grow and evolve. What matters is knowing where they are and having a plan."

Establish Credibility — Frameworks

Next 2 minutes
"Our assessment is built on the same frameworks AWS uses with enterprise customers globally:"
"First, the AWS Cloud Adoption Framework — specifically the Security Perspective. This gives us the organizational view — governance, identity, threat detection, infrastructure protection, incident response. It's how we measure security maturity."
"Second, the AWS Well-Architected Framework — Security Pillar. This is the workload-level lens. It looks at how your architecture decisions impact security. Together with CAF, we're covering both the big picture and the details."
"For your internet breakout specifically, we'll benchmark against the AWS Security Reference Architecture — this is AWS's prescriptive design for how network traffic should flow through centralized inspection, egress filtering, and segmented VPCs. It's the gold standard."
"And for configuration hardening, we'll use the CIS AWS Foundations Benchmark — the latest version. This gives us 39 automated controls that Security Hub can continuously monitor after we're done."
"So you're not just getting our opinion — you're getting an assessment grounded in industry-recognized standards."

Set Expectations — What They Get

Next 2 minutes
"Here's what you'll walk away with at the end of this engagement:"
  • "A scored assessment workbook — every area rated on a 1-5 maturity scale with Red, Amber, Green status. You'll see exactly where you're strong and where the gaps are."
  • "A findings tracker with severity ratings, business impact, and specific remediation steps — not generic advice, but actionable recommendations tailored to your environment."
  • "An executive summary dashboard your leadership can use to understand the security posture at a glance."
  • "And a prioritized remediation roadmap — because fixing everything at once isn't realistic. We'll help you sequence what matters most."

Make It Collaborative — Ask Them

Next 2 minutes
"Before we dive in, I'd love to hear from you:"
"What's driving this assessment? Is it a compliance requirement, a security concern, a leadership initiative, or are you planning changes to your architecture?"
(Let them talk. Listen. This tells you everything about their priorities.)
"Are there specific areas you're most concerned about or want us to focus on?"
(This makes them feel heard and gives you the priority order.)
"Who are the key stakeholders we should involve — network team, security team, identity team, operations?"
(This shows you respect their org structure and want to include the right people.)

Close the Intro — Transition to Assessment

Wrap-up
"Perfect. That's really helpful context. Here's how I'd like to structure our conversation today — we'll start from the foundation and work our way through layer by layer:"
"We'll begin with your account structure and governance — how your AWS environment is organized. Then we'll move into network architecture and the internet breakout path. From there, we'll look at the security controls on that path, identity and access, data protection, monitoring, and incident response."
"Each layer builds on the previous one, so by the end we'll have a complete picture."
"I'll be taking notes as we go and mapping everything to our assessment workbook. If at any point something isn't clear or you want to go deeper on a topic, just say so — this is your assessment."
"Ready to get started?"

Cheat Sheet — Trust Phrases to Drop Naturally

  • "We see this across many customers at your scale..."— normalizes their gaps
  • "That's actually a common pattern we encounter..."— removes shame
  • "The good news is this is straightforward to fix..."— gives hope
  • "AWS has a native service for exactly this..."— shows you know the platform
  • "Based on what we've seen work well for similar environments..."— positions you as experienced
  • "Let me show you what the reference architecture recommends here..."— grounds advice in AWS standards
  • "This is one of the highest-impact improvements you can make..."— helps them prioritize
  • "Your team has done solid work here..."— acknowledge what's working, don't just find problems

02 Speaking Notes

Full talk track for the call opening.

1. Set the Stage — Why We're Here

"Thank you for making time today. The purpose of this engagement is to perform an independent technical assessment of your AWS Cloud Internet Breakout environment — your network architecture, security controls, and operational readiness. Our goal is to give you a clear picture of where you stand today, where the gaps are, and a prioritized roadmap to close them."
"This isn't an audit. This is a partnership. We want to help you build confidence that your cloud environment is secure, resilient, and aligned with industry best practices."

2. Frameworks We're Using — Build Trust

AWS Cloud Adoption Framework (CAF) — Security Perspective

"Our assessment is grounded in the AWS Cloud Adoption Framework, specifically the Security Perspective. This is the same framework AWS uses with thousands of enterprise customers globally. It organizes security into 9 capabilities — from identity management to incident response — and gives us a structured way to measure your maturity."
Drop this: "The CAF Security Perspective covers Security Governance, Security Assurance, Identity & Access Management, Threat Detection, Vulnerability Management, Infrastructure Protection, Data Protection, Application Security, and Incident Response."

AWS Well-Architected Framework — Security Pillar

"We'll also be leveraging the AWS Well-Architected Framework, specifically the Security Pillar. This is AWS's own lens for evaluating workload-level architecture decisions. It covers 5 focus areas — Identity & Access Management, Detection, Infrastructure Protection, Data Protection, and Incident Response. Think of CAF as the organizational view and Well-Architected as the workload view — we're covering both."
Drop this: "The Well-Architected Tool is available free in the AWS Console. After our assessment, you can run ongoing reviews yourself."

AWS Security Reference Architecture (SRA)

"For your network and internet breakout design specifically, we're benchmarking against the AWS Security Reference Architecture. This is AWS's prescriptive multi-account architecture that defines how inbound, outbound, and inspection VPCs should be structured — with centralized Network Firewall, Transit Gateway routing, and dedicated security tooling accounts. This is the gold standard for how internet breakout should look."
Drop this: "The SRA recommends a dedicated Network account with three VPCs — Inbound, Outbound, and Inspection — with AWS Network Firewall in the inspection VPC handling all traffic."

CIS AWS Foundations Benchmark v5.0.0

"For compliance and configuration hardening, we're using the CIS AWS Foundations Benchmark version 5.0.0 — the latest version. This is an industry-accepted standard with clear, step-by-step controls. AWS Security Hub natively supports this benchmark, so after our assessment, you can enable automated compliance monitoring against these controls."
Drop this: "CIS v5.0.0 covers 39 automated controls across IAM, logging, monitoring, networking, and storage. Security Hub can continuously evaluate your environment against these."

NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)

"Where applicable, we'll also map findings to the NIST Cybersecurity Framework — Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover. Many of our customers need this mapping for regulatory or board-level reporting. It gives your leadership a common language to understand security posture."

ProServe Security Assessment Methodology

"Our assessment methodology follows the same approach used by AWS Professional Services across hundreds of enterprise engagements. We assess across 5 Core Epics aligned to the CAF Security Perspective — Identity & Access Management, Infrastructure Security, Data Protection, Detective Controls, and Incident Response. Each area is scored on a 1-5 maturity scale, giving you a quantifiable baseline."

3. What We'll Deliver — Set Expectations

"By the end of this engagement, you'll receive:"
  • A comprehensive assessment workbook with every control scored 1-5 and RAG-rated (Red/Amber/Green)
  • A findings tracker with severity, business impact, and remediation recommendations
  • An executive dashboard summarizing your security posture across all domains
  • A prioritized remediation roadmap with owners and target timelines
  • Mapping to CIS, NIST, and Well-Architected best practices
"This gives your team a clear, actionable plan — not just a list of problems."

4. How We'll Work — The Process

"Our approach has three phases:"
Phase 1 — Discovery We'll conduct structured interviews with your network, security, identity, and operations teams. We'll walk through your architecture, review configurations, and understand your current tooling.
Phase 2 — Analysis We'll analyze what we've gathered, score each area against the frameworks, identify gaps, and develop recommendations. Where possible, we'll use tools like Prowler, AWS Config, and Security Hub to validate findings.
Phase 3 — Readout & Roadmap We'll present findings to your team, walk through the prioritized recommendations, and align on next steps. This isn't a hand-off — we'll iterate with you.

5. What We Need From You — Ask

"To make this successful, we'll need:"
  • Access to your architecture diagrams (or we'll help you build them)
  • Time with your network, security, and identity teams (2-3 hours per domain)
  • Read-only access to your AWS environment for validation (Config, Security Hub, CloudTrail)
  • A point of contact to coordinate scheduling
"We respect your time. We'll come prepared with specific questions for each session so we're efficient."

6. Trust Builders — Drop These Naturally

On shared responsibility

"AWS secures the cloud infrastructure — the physical data centers, the hypervisor, the global network. Your responsibility is securing what you put IN the cloud — your configurations, your data, your access controls. Our job is to help you nail that 'in the cloud' piece."

On why this matters now

"The internet breakout path is the most critical attack surface in any cloud environment. It's where your workloads touch the public internet. Getting this right — centralized inspection, egress filtering, DDoS protection — is foundational to everything else."

On our approach

"We're not here to find fault. We're here to find opportunity. Every organization has gaps — what matters is knowing where they are and having a plan to close them."

On benchmarking

"We'll show you not just where you are, but where you should be. Our scoring is based on what we see across hundreds of AWS customers in similar industries and at similar scale."

7. Framework Quick Reference — Cheat Sheet

  • AWS CAF Security Perspective — 9 capabilities, organizational-level security maturity
  • AWS Well-Architected Security Pillar — 5 focus areas, workload-level architecture review
  • AWS Security Reference Architecture (SRA) — Prescriptive multi-account network + security design
  • CIS AWS Foundations Benchmark v5.0.0 — 39 automated configuration controls
  • NIST CSF — Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover (regulatory mapping)

03 AWS Services Reference

Field SA guide: discovery questions.

Strategy

  • How does the system support the customer's business? (e.g. CRM logs customer interactions and integrates with ERP for fulfillment)
  • How does the system scale as the business grows? What are the growth constraints? (e.g. servers, storage, people)
  • How does the system handle business continuity?
  • What are the technologies used in the system? (e.g. Oracle, OpenJDK, Javascript, Linux, VMWare)
  • How does the system integrate with other systems? (web hooks, integration broker, etc)
  • What are the peak performance metrics for the system? When does the system require peak performance? (e.g. maximum transactions per second, maximum tenants per cluster, etc)
  • What teams support the systems? (e.g. development, operations, DBA)
  • How does the team build and deploy the system? (e.g. Gradle, Jenkins, Ansible)
  • How do the teams test the systems? (e.g. manual, automated functional regression, unit tests, integration tests)
  • How does the team monitor the system for security, application, and performance incidents?
  • What are the system's incident response processes?
  • How do the team predict failure or service level violations? (e.g. AIOps)
  • How do you support the end users of the system? (e.g. ticketing systems, call/contact center)
  • How could the system generate more value if augmented with AI/ML?
  • Could the system's data provide insights to increase efficiency, drive growth via cross/upsell opportunities, etc.?

Technology Landscape

  • How does the technology organization serve the business? Are there dedicated teams for each business unit? Does a single team serve all the business units?
  • What regulatory constraints do you have? (e.g. HIPAA compliance, Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) audits, FINRA compliance, etc)
  • What drives the buy vs build decision? (e.g. customer buys accounting and HR systems but builds development tools)
  • Do you have an AI and ML strategy for augmenting or automating your business processes? (automating document workflows, information retrieval from unstructured data, etc)
  • Do you have a strategy to derive business insights from your data? (e.g. a "Customer 360" strategy by pulling data from CRM, customer success, and ERP system into a single view and data lake)
  • Do you have a strategy to monetize your data? (e.g. improving credit quality decisions using payment data, improving rating models through driver telematics, predictive maintenance program through IoT and service history)
  • What is your overall application integration strategy? (Enterprise service bus/integration broker (Mulesoft, Informatica, Boomi), point to point, etc.)
  • What is the software delivery process? (Scrum, Agile, XP, Waterfall)
  • What do you use for development tools, databases, application monitoring tools, security monitoring tools, continuous integration (CI), continuous delivery (CD)?
  • What percentage of technology infrastructure is in the cloud? What percentage is in AWS?
  • Does your IT strategy explicitly state cloud's role and how it is tied to your business objectives?
  • Where do you want to invest in infrastructure – innovation vs. application outcomes?
  • Are you aligning strategy on both cost out and innovation?

Security

  • What are the security compliance requirements? (SOC2, ISO 27001, HIPAA, etc)
  • Does your organization have a security and compliance program?
  • What tools or services are being used for security control?
  • What regulations does your organization need to comply with?
  • How would you characterize your organization's approach to security and compliance? Do you find it effective and efficient at managing risk?
  • To what extent are you incorporating data analytics into your operational risk management program?
  • Are there risk and compliance management practices that you feel would benefit from a more data driven approach?
  • What are the most resource intensive areas of your security and compliance program?
  • What are the main cost drivers, and where do you see opportunities for increased automation of manual processes?
  • At a high level, how would you characterize your organization's approach to security and compliance? How integrated and consistent would you say it is across lines of business?

Opportunity Information

  • Do you currently have central pane of glass view into your AWS security posture?
  • Do you currently or plan on storing or processing protected data?
  • What 3rd party products are you currently using on premise / on AWS?

  • Can you describe your DR processes in general?
  • How often do you conduct DR test or exercises?
  • Can you tell me your most recent DR experience?
    • Areas of focus: Policies
    • Procedures
    • Organization
    • RTOs and RPOs (business vs technical)
    • Date of DRP last updated
    • DR exercise frequency and last exercise date
    • Are staff trained on DRP
    • Does everyone understand their roles within the DRP
    • Are there any SLA considerations, if so, what are they?
    • Are there any security considerations, if so, what are they?
  • What is the backup solution being used in your environment?
  • How much data are you protecting in your environment?
  • Where are you storing the backup data today?
  • Can you describe your backup processes in general?
  • How often do you run backup jobs?
  • What are the main applications and data type you are backing up?
  • Are you sending your backup data offsite or to the cloud?
  • Is your DR based on restoring from backup data?
    • Areas of focus: Backup architecture
    • Backup policy (source data, frequency, retention etc.)
    • Backup full capacity (breakdown by files, databases, applications, etc.)
    • Backup medium: disk, tape, cloud, offsite?
    • Backup window
    • How often do you test recovery from backup?
    • Are there procedures for recovery from backup?
    • Organization structure
    • Do you offer SLAs to business groups?

04 Cloud Internet Breakout

Assessment interview playbook.

How to use this: Start from Layer 0 (Foundation) and work your way down. Each layer builds on the previous one. The questions are designed to uncover gaps naturally — the customer's answers at each layer will tell you where to probe deeper. Map every answer back to the workbook tab noted in brackets.

Layer 0 — Foundation & Account Structure

"Before we look at any traffic flows, let's understand how your AWS environment is organized."

Opening Questions

  • Can you walk us through your AWS account structure? How many accounts are you running, and how are they organized?
  • Are you using AWS Control Tower or AWS Organizations? If so, how are your OUs structured?
    • Do you have separate OUs for Security, Shared Services, Workloads (Prod/Dev/Staging), Sandbox?
  • Is there a dedicated Network/Shared Services account that centralizes your internet breakout?
  • Are you using AWS Landing Zone or Control Tower guardrails? Which guardrails are enabled — detective, preventive, or both?
  • Who manages the root account? Is MFA enforced on root? Is root usage monitored?
  • Do you have Service Control Policies (SCPs) in place? What do they restrict?
    • Example: Do SCPs prevent disabling CloudTrail, leaving certain regions, or creating public S3 buckets?
What you're mapping: [Tab 8 — Compliance & Governance: Account Structure] + [Tab 4 — IAM: SCPs]
Why this matters first: If the foundation is shaky (no multi-account strategy, no guardrails, no centralized networking account), everything downstream is at risk. This sets the tone.

Layer 1 — Network Architecture & Topology

"Now let's understand how your network is designed and how traffic flows."

VPC Design

  • How many VPCs do you have, and what's the segmentation strategy? (by workload, environment, business unit?)
  • What's your CIDR planning approach? Any overlapping ranges? How much room for growth?
  • Are workloads deployed across multiple Availability Zones?
  • Do you have a dedicated Inspection VPC or Egress VPC for centralized internet breakout?

Internet Breakout Path (THE CORE)

Walk me through the exact traffic flow for a workload that needs to reach the internet:

  • Workload → ? → ? → ? → Internet
  • Is it: Workload → NAT Gateway → AWS Network Firewall → Internet Gateway → Internet?
  • Or is each VPC breaking out independently?
  • Is your breakout model centralized (via Transit Gateway + shared egress VPC) or distributed (per-VPC NAT/IGW)?
  • For inbound traffic: Internet → ? → ? → Workload — what does that path look like?

Transit & Routing

  • Are you using AWS Transit Gateway? How are route tables configured?
  • Are there any blackhole routes for security isolation?
  • Is route propagation enabled, or are routes managed statically?

DNS

  • Are you using Route 53 for DNS? Private hosted zones?
  • Is Route 53 DNS Firewall enabled to block DNS exfiltration or malicious domains?

Hybrid Connectivity

  • Do you have Direct Connect or Site-to-Site VPN back to on-premises?
  • How does on-prem traffic reach the internet — through AWS or through their own breakout?
What you're mapping: [Tab 2 — Network Architecture: ALL categories]
Pro tip: Ask them to share or screen-share their architecture diagram. If they don't have one, that's already a finding.

Layer 2 — Security Controls on the Breakout Path

"Now that we understand the traffic flow, let's look at what's inspecting and protecting that traffic."

Network Firewall

  • Are you using AWS Network Firewall? Where is it deployed in the traffic path?
  • Are you using stateful rules, stateless rules, or both? What's the rule order (strict or default)?
  • Is Network Firewall logging enabled? Where do logs go — S3, CloudWatch, or both?
  • Are you inspecting BOTH egress AND ingress traffic through the firewall?

Security Groups & NACLs

  • How are Security Groups managed? Is there a standard baseline?
  • Are there any rules allowing 0.0.0.0/0 inbound on SSH (22) or RDP (3389)?
  • Are NACLs used as an additional layer, or just left at default allow-all?

Egress Filtering (Critical for Internet Breakout)

  • Is outbound traffic restricted to known-good destinations, or is it open?
  • Do you have domain-based allowlisting for egress? (e.g., only allow *.amazonaws.com, specific SaaS endpoints)
  • How do you prevent data exfiltration via DNS tunneling or HTTPS to unauthorized endpoints?

WAF & DDoS

  • Is AWS WAF deployed in front of any public-facing endpoints? Which managed rule groups?
  • Are you on AWS Shield Standard or Advanced?

Encryption in Transit

  • Is TLS 1.2+ enforced on all endpoints? How are certificates managed — ACM?
  • Is there end-to-end encryption, or does TLS terminate at the load balancer?
What you're mapping: [Tab 3 — Security Controls: ALL categories]
Key red flags to listen for: "We haven't restricted egress yet" / "Security groups are managed per team" / "We don't inspect outbound traffic"

Layer 3 — Identity & Access Management

"Who has access to modify this network and security infrastructure?"

  • How do engineers authenticate to AWS? IAM users, IAM Identity Center (SSO), or federated via an IdP?
  • Is MFA enforced for all human access? What type — hardware tokens or virtual?
  • How are IAM policies structured? Do you follow least-privilege? Any policies with *:*?
  • Are there permission boundaries in place for delegated admin roles?
  • How are secrets managed — Secrets Manager, Parameter Store, or (red flag) hardcoded/environment variables?
  • How often are access keys rotated? Are there any long-lived access keys?
  • Who has permission to modify Security Groups, NACLs, route tables, and Network Firewall rules?
What you're mapping: [Tab 4 — Identity & Access Management]

Layer 4 — Data Protection

"Let's talk about how data is protected at rest and in transit across this environment."

  • Is encryption at rest enabled for all storage — EBS, S3, RDS, DynamoDB?
  • Are you using AWS-managed keys or Customer Managed Keys (CMKs) in KMS?
  • Is KMS key rotation enabled? What's the rotation policy?
  • For S3 specifically:
    • Is S3 Block Public Access enabled at the account level?
    • Is versioning and MFA Delete enabled on critical buckets?
    • Are S3 access logs enabled?
  • Do you have a data classification strategy? Are you using Amazon Macie?
  • What's your backup strategy? AWS Backup? Cross-region replication? Have you tested recovery?
What you're mapping: [Tab 5 — Data Protection]

Layer 5 — Monitoring, Detection & Visibility

"Can you see what's happening in your environment right now?"

  • Is CloudTrail enabled in ALL regions, including regions you don't use?
  • Is CloudTrail a multi-account, organization-level trail?
  • Is Amazon GuardDuty enabled across all accounts and regions? Who reviews the findings?
  • Is AWS Security Hub enabled? Which standards are turned on — CIS, AWS Foundational, PCI DSS?
  • Are VPC Flow Logs enabled on all VPCs, subnets, and key ENIs?
    • Where do they go? Are you analyzing them (Athena, SIEM)?
  • Is Amazon Inspector running for network reachability and vulnerability assessments?
  • Do you have CloudWatch alarms for security-critical events?
    • Root login, unauthorized API calls, Security Group changes, route table modifications?
  • Are AWS Config rules enabled? Any conformance packs deployed?
What you're mapping: [Tab 6 — Monitoring & Detection]
This layer is where most customers have the biggest gaps — they have the services but aren't actively reviewing findings.

Layer 6 — Incident Response Readiness

"If something goes wrong right now — a breach, a misconfiguration, an exfiltration attempt — what happens?"

  • Do you have a documented incident response plan specific to AWS?
  • Are there runbooks or playbooks for common scenarios?
    • Compromised EC2 instance, leaked credentials, unauthorized S3 access, DDoS attack?
  • Can you isolate a compromised resource quickly? (Security Group isolation, NACL deny, credential revocation)
  • Do you have a forensics capability — isolated VPC, snapshot procedures, evidence chain of custody?
  • What are your RTO/RPO targets? Have you tested disaster recovery?
  • After an incident, is there a lessons-learned / COE process?
What you're mapping: [Tab 7 — Incident Response]

Layer 7 — Compliance & Governance

"Finally, let's look at the governance framework around all of this."

  • Have you run the CIS AWS Foundations Benchmark against your environment? What's the compliance percentage?
  • Have you done an AWS Well-Architected Security Pillar review?
  • Is there a tagging strategy enforced via tag policies? What are the mandatory tags?
  • How do you handle audit and evidence collection — AWS Audit Manager?
  • Are there regular security reviews or assessments scheduled?
  • How do you track remediation of findings — is there a process, or is it ad hoc?
What you're mapping: [Tab 8 — Compliance & Governance]

Closing the Conversation

After going through all layers, wrap up with:

  • "Based on what we've discussed, I'll document our findings in the assessment workbook and score each area."
  • "We'll provide you with a RAG-rated view — Red for critical gaps, Amber for partial implementation, Green for areas that meet best practices."
  • "The deliverable will include specific remediation recommendations with owners and target dates."
  • "Are there any areas we didn't cover that you'd like us to assess?"
